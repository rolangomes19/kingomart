## Q1. How much revenue do marketplace ads bring, and what share is top-of-page or homepage placement?

### Takeaway
Ads are big and fast growing. Amazon ads were about 10% of Amazon revenue in 2025 ($68.6B). Allegro ads were about 2% of GMV in Poland in Q2 2025. No marketplace publishes the share that comes from homepage or top-of-page banners. Sponsored product search ads are the main driver, not banners.

### Cited Findings

**Revenue size**
- Amazon advertising revenue: $56.22B in 2024, $68.63B in 2025 (+22%). Q1 2026: $17.24B (+24%). Source: [Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/stats/amazon-advertising-services-sales)
- Amazon Q4 2025 ads: $21.3B (+23% year on year). Sponsored Products was "the largest contributor" in Q4. Source: [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/amazons-ad-revenue-hits-21-3b-as-prime-video-reaches-315m-viewers/)
- Amazon 2025 net sales about $717B. Ads were roughly 9.6% of that. Source: [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/amazons-69b-ad-business-gets-one-sentence-in-jassys-2025-letter/)
- Amazon ad total includes Prime Video ads (16 countries) and DSP display, not only marketplace on-site slots. Source: [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/amazons-ad-revenue-hits-21-3b-as-prime-video-reaches-315m-viewers/)
- Allegro (Poland), Q2 2025: advertising grew 30.6% year on year and equalled 2.0% of GMV. Management calls it "margin accretive". Source: [Allegro Q2 2025 results presentation](https://about.allegro.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Results-Presentation-Q2-2025.pdf)
- Allegro Q2 2025: Polish revenue +18.1%, "boosted by advertising". Polish GMV grew 9.8%. Source: [Allegro Q2 2025 press release](https://about.allegro.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Allegro-Q2-2025-Press-Release.pdf)
- Cdiscount, H1 2026 estimates (30 July 2026, not audited): Cdiscount Advertising revenue +20.3% (period not stated). Marketplace was 72% of product GMV. Marketplace commissions +11.8% in Q2. Source: [Ecommerce Nation](https://www.ecommerce-nation.fr/cdiscount-s1-2026-rentabilite-retail-media-marketplace/)
- Zalando does not publish ZMS revenue. Its retail media lead says: "Wir kommunizieren keine Umsatzzahlen zu ZMS." ZMS is up in the "zweistelligen %bereich" in 2025 to date. Source: [Textilwirtschaft](https://www.textilwirtschaft.de/fashion-management/so-sieht-die-retail-media-strategie-von-zalando-aus-84677)
- Zalando 2025 annual report: GMV excludes ZMS and service fees. Those sit in revenue only. The CEO letter says the Partner business is "strengthened" by retail media. Source: [Zalando SE Annual Report 2025 (copy on Bundestag lobby register)](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/media/47/74/795619/zalando-se_annual-report-2025_compressed.pdf)
- Zalando ZMS: "2,400 active brand partners" in 2024. Page gives no revenue. Source: [Zalando corporate](https://corporate.zalando.com/en/node/10807)
- European retail media grew 22.1% in 2024 versus 6.1% for all advertising. Sponsored Products is the main driver. Source: [PPC Land, citing IAB Europe](https://ppc.land/retail-media-surge-sponsored-products-drive-22-growth-in-european-market/)
- About 80% of 2025 retail media spend goes to retailers' own sites and apps. On-site ad margins often exceed 70% (BCG). Amazon holds about three quarters of retail media spend. Source: [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/upper-limits-of-retail-media-s-on-site-monetization-coming-focus)
- Alibaba International Digital Commerce revenue was $5.17B in the quarter to 31 Dec 2024 (+32%). The source does not split ads from memberships. Source: [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/02/20/alibaba-revenue-q3-fy25/)

**How banner slots are sold**
- Display banners are sold per 1,000 impressions (CPM). Homepage banners rate high. Above-the-fold hero slots and interstitials rate highest. Source: [Hello Retail](https://helloretail.com/en/learn/retail-media-pricing/)
- Retailers can bundle a homepage banner, search slots and email into one monthly flat package. Source: [Hello Retail](https://helloretail.com/en/learn/retail-media-pricing/)
- Sponsored products are sold per click (CPC), through keyword auctions. Cdiscount: sellers bid in "real-time auctions". Source: [Journal du Net](https://www.journaldunet.com/retail/1514005-six-formats-publicitaires-chez-cdiscount-lequel-choisir/)
- Cdiscount live shopping is priced "de 5 000 à 50 000 euros". The JDN article gives no CPC, CPM or homepage banner price. Source: [Journal du Net](https://www.journaldunet.com/retail/1514005-six-formats-publicitaires-chez-cdiscount-lequel-choisir/)
- Alibaba.com P4P: merchants bid on keywords to raise visibility. The source is promotional and gives no price or homepage detail. Source: [XTransfer](https://www.xtransfer.com/newsroom/p4p-marketing-on-alibaba)
- bol: ad services let partners "highlight their products". About one in four partners used them in July 2021. No revenue or pricing given. Source: [RetailDetail](https://www.retaildetail.eu/news/general/bolcom-becoming-advertising-platform-well-sales-platform)

**Who pays (brand or supplier funded)**
- Retail media is paid by brands (advertisers). IAB Europe defines Sponsored Products as "a brand pays to promote a singular product". Source: [PPC Land, citing IAB Europe](https://ppc.land/retail-media-surge-sponsored-products-drive-22-growth-in-european-market/)
- Cdiscount: sellers pay for sponsored products, sponsored brands and Google Shopping. Source: [Journal du Net](https://www.journaldunet.com/retail/1514005-six-formats-publicitaires-chez-cdiscount-lequel-choisir/)
- Hello Retail frames advertisers (brands) as payers under CPM, CPC and flat fee. The retailer takes the revenue. Source: [Hello Retail](https://helloretail.com/en/learn/retail-media-pricing/)
- Amazon CEO letter 2025: ad offerings "deliver strong returns for brands". Source: [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/amazons-69b-ad-business-gets-one-sentence-in-jassys-2025-letter/)
- Zalando CEO letter: partners get advertising tools and prominent placement. This links partner growth to platform profit. Zalando does not say brands fund banners. Source: [Zalando SE Annual Report 2025](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/media/47/74/795619/zalando-se_annual-report-2025_compressed.pdf)

**Tension between ad revenue and shopping conversion**
- FTC alleges Amazon accepted more "defects" (irrelevant ads) to raise ad revenue, and that this worsened search. Amazon called the claim "grossly misleading". This is an allegation, not a finding. Source: [Retail Dive, 7 Nov 2023](https://www.retaildive.com/news/amazon-ftc-antitrust-lawsuit-defect-ads-Jeff-Bezos/698930/)
- Pentaleap (Nov 2024, via eMarketer): paid results on 99% of Amazon and Walmart product searches, with 20+ sponsored listings per page. Retailers must weigh ad income against shopper fatigue. Source: [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/upper-limits-of-retail-media-s-on-site-monetization-coming-focus)
- Flipkart field test (1.2M users, 8 days, 16 to 26% ad load). Electronics: ads did worse than the organic listings they displaced, net sales neutral. Clothing: ads converted better, sales rose. Short term only. Source: [Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/research-brief/online-marketplaces-retail-media)
- Advertisers question ROI of ads "so close to the bottom of the purchase funnel". Source: [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/upper-limits-of-retail-media-s-on-site-monetization-coming-focus)

### Inferences
- A top banner earns money by flat fee or CPM and the buyer pays nothing. A banner also costs space that organic product cards would use. The test is net sales, not ad income.
- For a B2B food and goods buyer, the Flipkart electronics result (spec-driven buying, ads no better than organic) is the closer analogy than fashion. This is an inference from one study in India, B2C.
- If KingoMart runs a banner for its own lanes (open departures), it is an own campaign. If a supplier pays for it, DSA Art 26 and French LCEN Art 20 labels apply (see Q2). No source found where a marketplace states "the banner is a supplier funding channel".

### Gaps
- No marketplace splits ad revenue by placement (homepage vs search vs category). Share of top-of-page or homepage banners: not found.
- Zalando ZMS revenue: not published. Statista figure is paywalled (values masked).
- bol advertising revenue: not found. The only bol source is from 2021.
- Cdiscount Advertising absolute revenue: not found. Period of the +20.3% not stated.
- Metro marketplace: the Metro page lists no ads or seller fees. No source on Metro ads found.
- Alibaba.com: no official source on P4P price, homepage slots or the ad share of revenue. Alibaba filings were found in search (SEC 20-F) but not read.
- Amazon vendor co-op or supplier-funded promotion terms: not searched. No primary statement found.
- Statement by any marketplace that its banner is a brand or supplier funding channel: not found.

## Q2. What labels are mandatory on a sponsored banner or sponsored product card in the EU and France?

### Takeaway
Three layers. (1) DSA Art 26(1): every ad on an online platform must show it is an ad, who it is for, who paid, and the main targeting parameters. (2) UCPD Annex I point 11a and Art 7(4a) plus the French Code de la consommation: paid search placement must be disclosed, but these protect consumers only. (3) French LCEN Art 20 asks for a clear ad mark and the advertiser identity, and is not shown to be limited to consumers.

### Cited Findings

**DSA Art 26 (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Grade: SECONDARY (mirror text). Applies to B2B and B2C.**
- Art 26(1) applies to providers of online platforms that "present advertisements". For each ad and each user, in real time: (a) it is an advertisement, "including through prominent markings"; (b) the person on whose behalf it is presented; (c) the person who paid, if different; (d) meaningful information on the main parameters used to target the user, and how to change them. Source: [Springlex, Art 26](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-26/); same content at [eu-digital-services-act.com](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_26.html)
- Art 26(2): platform must give users a way to declare their content is commercial communication. Source: [Springlex, Art 26](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-26/)
- Art 26(3): no ads based on profiling that uses special categories of data (GDPR Art 9(1)). Source: [Springlex, Art 26](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-26/)
- Art 3(r): "advertisement" means "information designed to promote the message of a legal or natural person". The tool quote was cut here. The full definition (paid, on the online interface) is NOT VERIFIED in this session. Secondary source for the cut part: none read. Source: [Springlex, Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 3(i): "online platform" is "a hosting service that, at the request of a recipient of the service, stores and disseminates information to the public". Source: [Springlex, Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-3/)
- Art 19(1): the section that holds Arts 20 to 28 (incl. 25, 26, 27) "shall not apply" to online platforms that are micro or small enterprises (Rec. 2003/361/EC). Exception: Art 24(3). Exemption lasts 12 months after the provider grows out of it. Source: [Springlex, Art 19](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-19/)
- Art 25(1): no interface design that deceives or manipulates users or "materially distorts" free, informed choice. Art 25(2): does not apply to practices covered by Directive 2005/29/EC or GDPR. Source: [Springlex, Art 25](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-25/)

**UCPD 2005/29/EC as amended by Omnibus 2019/2161. Grade: PRIMARY (EUR-Lex via fetch). B2C only.**
- Annex I point 11a: "Providing search results in response to a consumer's online search query without clearly disclosing any paid advertisement" (or payment for higher ranking) is always unfair. Source: [Directive 2019/2161, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L2161)
- UCPD Art 7(4a): where consumers can search for products from different traders, the main ranking parameters and their relative importance must be given in a dedicated section reachable from the results page. Not for search engines covered by P2B. Source: [Directive 2019/2161, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L2161)
- Directive 2011/83/EU Art 6a (new): a consumer marketplace must disclose the main ranking parameters, whether the seller is a trader, and how obligations are shared. Source: [Directive 2019/2161, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L2161)

**P2B Regulation (EU) 2019/1150. Grade: PRIMARY (EUR-Lex via fetch).**
- Art 5(1): terms must set out "the main parameters determining ranking" and why they matter. Source: [Regulation 2019/1150, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019R1150)
- Art 5(3): if ranking can be influenced by direct or indirect payment, the provider must describe that option and its effect. Source: [Regulation 2019/1150, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019R1150)
- Art 7(1): describe any differentiated treatment that favours own offers. Source: [Regulation 2019/1150, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019R1150)
- Scope: Art 1(2) covers services that serve business users who offer to consumers located in the Union. Art 2(2) "online intermediation services" are those that "allow business users to offer goods or services to consumers". Source: [Regulation 2019/1150, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019R1150)

**France. Grade: NOT VERIFIED from Legifrance (pages failed to load). Secondary sources named.**
- Code de la consommation L111-7: platform operators owe consumers "une obligation d'information loyale, claire et transparente" on referencing, ranking and delisting. Secondary: [actu-juridique](https://www.actu-juridique.fr/ntic-numerique-medias-presse/les-principales-dispositions-du-decret-n-2017-1434-du-29-septembre-2017-relatif-aux-obligations-dinformation-des-operateurs-de-plates-formes-numeriques/)
- Decree 2017-1434 (D111-7): a section reachable from every page covers default ranking criteria and any capital link or payment that affects ranking. Each ranked result linked to a contract, capital link or payment must be marked near the offer. Fines up to EUR 75,000 (person) and EUR 375,000 (company) under L131-4. Secondary: [INC](https://www.inc-conso.fr/content/informations-par-les-plateformes-en-ligne-ce-qui-change-au-1er-janvier-2018)
- Omnibus transposition (Ordonnance 2021-1734): L121-4 holds deemed-misleading practices. Showing search results without telling the consumer the platform was paid for a better ranking is one. The point number in L121-4 was not given by the source. Ranking parameters are "substantial information" (L121-3 and L121-4 area). Secondary: [INC Omnibus table](https://inc-conso.fr/sites/default/files/pdf/tableau-omnibus-inc-2022_0.pdf)
- L111-7 I 2° defines the marketplace operator as a professional who provides an online marketplace "aux consommateurs". Secondary: [INC Omnibus table](https://inc-conso.fr/sites/default/files/pdf/tableau-omnibus-inc-2022_0.pdf)
- LCEN (Loi 2004-575) Art 20: online advertising "quelle que soit sa forme" must show its commercial nature and the identity of the person on whose behalf it is made. A Paris court (18 June 2013) required clear terms like "publicité" or "annonce" and faulted small pale grey type. Secondary: [CMS Law](https://cms.law/fr/fra/a-la-une/les-publicites-sur-internet-doivent-elles-forcement-dire-leur-nom). The source does not say the rule is limited to consumers.
- Price reduction: announced prior price = lowest price in the previous 30 days (Directive 98/6/EC Art 6a; French L112-1-1 per INC). Source: [Directive 2019/2161, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L2161); [INC Omnibus table](https://inc-conso.fr/sites/default/files/pdf/tableau-omnibus-inc-2022_0.pdf). French rules are framed for consumers: [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/professionnels-quelles-sont-vos-obligations-en-matiere-daffichage-des-prix)

### Inferences
- Safe label set for a sponsored banner or card: the word "Sponsored" (FR "Sponsorisé" or "Publicité") in clear type, the advertiser name, the payer name if different, and a link "Why am I seeing this". This meets DSA 26(1) in spirit and LCEN Art 20. The wording "Sponsorise" is not mandated by any text found. DSA says "prominent markings", not a fixed word.
- Art 26(1)(d) targeting info is small for a non-personalised banner. If the banner is the same for all buyers, the "main parameters" answer is "none, shown to all". This is my inference.

### Gaps
- Exact current Legifrance text of L111-7, L121-4 (point number), L112-1-1, LCEN Art 20: not read.
- DSA Art 3(r) full wording (paid, on the interface): cut off in the tool output.
- No DSA or French source found that fixes a mandatory word such as "Sponsorisé".
- Dark patterns in French law beyond DSA Art 25 and UCPD: not searched.

## Q3. Do these rules differ for a B2B-only marketplace? Which apply to KingoMart?

### Takeaway
Most "marketplace" rules are written for consumers and do not apply to a B2B-only storefront: P2B, UCPD/Omnibus, CRD Art 6a, DSA Arts 30 to 32, French L111-7 and the 30-day price rule. Two rules likely do apply: DSA Arts 26 and 27 (if KingoMart is an online platform and not a micro or small firm) and French LCEN Art 20 (ad labelling). French Code de commerce L441-1 applies to B2B price terms.

### Cited Findings

**What a B2B storefront shown only to French business buyers must label and disclose**

**Other facts**
- L441-1 CGV: sellers may issue different CGV per buyer category. Fines up to EUR 15,000 (person) and EUR 75,000 (company). Source: [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/gerer-sa-comptabilite-et-ses-demarches/conditions-generales-de-vente-entre)
- DSA Art 29 exempts micro and small online platforms from Arts 30 to 32 too, for 12 months after status loss. Source: [Springlex Art 29](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-29/)
- DSA Art 30 (for platforms in scope): collect trader name, address, contact, ID, payment account, register number, and a self-certification; show name, address and self-certification where the product is presented. Not required for a B2B-only platform per the Freshfields reading. Source: [eu-digital-services-act.com Art 30](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_30.html)

### Inferences
- If a supplier pays for a banner on KingoMart, the banner needs a "Sponsored" mark, the advertiser name and the payer name (DSA 26(1) and LCEN 20). If KingoMart advertises its own lanes, the safe course is a label "KingoMart offer" so that the buyer can tell it is not a supplier ad. This is a design choice, not a stated legal rule.
- KingoMart sells to approved business buyers only and guests see the catalog. Whether that counts as dissemination "to the public" (DSA Art 3(i)) is a legal question I could not resolve. A gated storefront may argue it is not public. Ask counsel.
- KingoMart's size (micro or small under Rec. 2003/361/EC) is unknown. If it is micro or small, Arts 26 and 27 do not bind it by law (Art 19). Labels are still best practice and LCEN Art 20 still applies.
- B2B buyers have no 30-day lowest price duty. A "was/now" price on a B2B card is not barred by the sources read. It still risks a misleading-claim argument under general French commercial law, which I did not research.
- Sample-data labels in the prototype stay needed: they stop fake offers being read as real.

### Gaps
- No source states outright that Arts 30 to 32 exclude B2B-only platforms. The Freshfields page does not address it. The exclusion is read from the Section 4 heading.
- DSA Art 26 scope for B2B: the mirror text says "recipients of the service". Commission guidance on B2B platforms: not found.
- French B2B ad and price rules in Code de commerce L442-1 (abusive practices) and general B2B misleading advertising (Code de la consommation L121-... does not cover B2B; Code de commerce 1240 civil route): not researched.
- Whether L441-1 requires a public price list on a storefront: only "communicate on request" found.
- CJEU C-330/23 (Aldi Süd, 30-day rule): cited in project index, not re-read here.

## Q4. What does the DSA say about ranking and recommender explanation on a storefront?

### Takeaway
Art 27: an online platform that uses recommender systems must state the main parameters in its terms, in plain language, with the reasons for their weight and any option to change them. If it offers several ranking options, it must give a tool to switch, where the content is ranked. Paid ranking is covered by Art 26 (label) and, for consumer services, by P2B, UCPD and French law.

### Cited Findings
- Art 27(1): terms and conditions must set out in plain, intelligible language "the main parameters used in their recommender systems", and any options to change or influence them. Source: [eu-digital-services-act.com Art 27](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_27.html)
- Art 27(2): the parameters must explain why information is suggested. They must cover "the criteria which are most significant" and the reasons for their relative weight. Source: [eu-digital-services-act.com Art 27](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_27.html)
- Art 27(3): where several options set the order of information, the platform must give a tool to choose and change the option at any time, directly where the content is ranked. Source: [eu-digital-services-act.com Art 27](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_27.html)
- Art 3(s): recommender system means "a fully or partially automated system used by an online platform to suggest in its online interface specific information". Source: [Springlex Art 3](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-3/)
- Recital 70: parameters should include the most important criteria "and the reasons for their importance". Source: [EUR-Lex DSA recitals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065)
- Art 19(1) exempts micro and small platforms from this section (which holds Art 27). Source: [Springlex Art 19](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-19/)
- P2B Art 5 is the parallel rule for ranking, but only where business users sell to consumers. Source: [EUR-Lex P2B](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019R1150)

### Inferences
- A storefront with a default sort ("Recommended") and other sorts (price, newest, departure date) is in the Art 27(3) case. It needs a visible sort control next to the list.
- Minimum copy to publish in the terms: "Default order uses: availability, departure date, price per unit, supplier rating, paid placement (if any)". The real parameters must be KingoMart's real ones. I do not know them.
- If a paid or sponsored card sits among organic cards, mark it on the card (Art 26) and list "paid placement" among the ranking parameters in the terms (Art 27(1), P2B-style practice).

### Gaps
- EUR-Lex DSA article text (Arts 26, 27, 30) could not be read; the tool returned recitals only. Article quotes come from mirrors.
- Commission guidance or case law on how detailed Art 27 parameters must be: not searched.
- Whether Art 27 requires a "non-profiling" ranking option: that is Art 38 and applies only to very large platforms. Not read.
- Whether a B2B gated storefront counts as an "online platform" at all: open legal question (see Q3).
